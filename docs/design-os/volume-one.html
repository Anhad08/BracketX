Volume One Beyond UI

The Streamatrix Design OS

Streamatrix is not a set of screens. It is one instrument, and an instrument has a behaviour before it has a look. This volume defines that behaviour: how things move, how they sound, what the cursor promises, what happens in the second after you commit, and what the product says when something goes wrong at 19:59 on a live night.

Everything here is operable, not illustrated. The sounds are synthesised as you press them. The cursors are the real cursors. The eight moments run. If a specimen misbehaves in this document, the specification is wrong — not the drawing of it.

Doctrine

Seven laws

These are not principles. Principles are aspirational and settle nothing. These are laws: each one decides a specific argument that will otherwise be re-litigated in every review, and each is falsifiable — you can point at a screen and say it was broken.

L1
Meaning never waits for motion. Any change that carries broadcast consequence is committed on the input frame. Animation reports what already happened; it is never in the path of it. If an animation were removed, the product would still be correct — only less legible.
L2
Attack is fast, release is slow. Things arrive in 90–180 ms and leave in 240–900 ms. Physical objects behave this way and it is why the interface feels like it has mass. A symmetric fade is the sound of a web page.
L3
Colour is a claim about the world. Red means it is on air or it cannot be undone. Amber means the system needs a person. Teal means cued, not live. Green means healthy. Nothing else may use these hues, ever, including for emphasis, charts, illustration or brand. Identity is a neutral.
L4
State is shown by light, not by fill. Active things illuminate — a lamp, an edge, a glow. They do not get a coloured background plate. A control room is dark with points of light in it, and that is legible across a room at 3 a.m. in a way a highlighted row is not.
L5
Elevation is earned. Three levels: chassis, panel, control. Only controls and true overlays cast shadows. A panel inside a panel is a seam, not another raised card. When everything is raised, nothing is.
L6
Never draw a control for something the engine cannot do. No greyed-out future features, no empty Video tab, no placeholder waveform. A control that cannot act is a lie, and on a live night it is a dangerous one. Capability ships with its control or neither ships.
L7
Every surface answers "what now?". There is no dead state. Loading says how much and how long. Empty says what to do and offers to do it. Error says what broke, what it means on air, and the one action that fixes it. A spinner alone is an unfinished screen.
The test If you cannot name which law a decision serves, it is decoration — and decoration in a control room is a distraction with a cost measured in seconds during a live show.

Motion

3 curves · 6 durations

Three curves, and Volume One adds no fourth. A fourth curve is almost always a designer papering over a wrong duration. Each has a job that the others cannot do, and each is named after what it feels like rather than what it computes.

Durationsnothing between these values
TokenValueCurveApplies to
instant0 ms—Anything with broadcast consequence: the cut, tally, arm, disarm. Law 1.

decay900 msweightRelease only: tally cooling, off-air fade, closure. Never an entrance.
Stagger 40 ms between siblings, capped at 6 items — beyond that the last one feels forgotten. Travel 6–10 px. Anything further reads as a page, not a panel.

Do

  • Move one property where possible — usually opacity plus a single axis.
  • Let the origin explain the motion: a docked panel slides from its dock edge.
  • Stagger siblings so the eye is led; never animate a grid all at once.
  • Interrupt cleanly — a new input cancels the running animation from its current value.

Never

  • Bounce, overshoot or elastic easing. Broadcast equipment does not wobble.
  • Animate a number's value. Counters change; they do not roll like a fruit machine.
  • Loop anything ambient. A moving thing in peripheral vision reads as an alarm.
  • Animate on data arrival — only on user intent. Data arrives constantly.
04

Sound

9 voices · synthesised

Sound is off by default and remembered per operator, because a gallery has its own audio discipline and an unexpected noise on a live desk is a fault, not a delight. When it is on, it behaves like hardware: short, dry, mechanical, with no melody and no personality. Nothing is cute. Nothing is a marimba.

Every voice below is generated live in this page — no samples, no files. That is deliberate: it proves the palette can be produced deterministically at any sample rate, and it means the specification cannot drift from the asset.

Law of the voice A sound may confirm that something happened. A sound may never be the only way to know it happened, and a sound may never be required to complete a task.

05

Cursor

hover each cell

The cursor is the only element that is always under the operator's attention, and in a precision tool it is a readout: it states what the next click will do before the click. Streamatrix draws its own rather than borrowing the OS set, because the OS set has no vocabulary for cue, take, or drop-into-timeline.

Every cursor is a 24 px monochrome mark with a one-pixel dark keyline so it survives on a white title card and on a black well. Hotspots are exact. There is no drop shadow — a cursor with a shadow floats above the work, and this one belongs to it.

Cursor setmove your pointer into a cell
Rule The cursor changes on capability, not on hover styling. If the cursor changes, a click must do something.

Material & Light

4 materials · 1 light

There is one light source, high and slightly left, and it never moves. Every highlight, seam and shadow in the product is derived from it. This is the single decision that makes an interface read as an object rather than a diagram, and it is also the one most often broken — by a card that catches light from below, or a shadow that falls the wrong way.

Space & Type

9 steps · 6 sizes

A 4 pt grid with nine steps. There is no value between them, and “it looked better at 15” is not an argument — it is a sign the wrong step was chosen. Six type sizes, each with its tracking bound to it: tighter as it grows, looser as it shrinks, which is what optical sizing does for a typeface that has it, and this one does not.

Open decision

All three roles currently resolve to the operating system's UI face. That is honest — it is what the product ships — but it is also the single largest remaining gap between this design and a flagship one. A licensed grotesque with true optical sizing and a matched mono would lift the whole product further than any additional material work. Until it is commissioned, this document does not pretend otherwise.

Language

words are components

Streamatrix speaks the way a competent engineer speaks to a director during a show: plainly, in the fewest words, leading with the consequence. It never apologises, never says “oops”, never exclaims, and never uses the word “error”.

Streamatrix says

  • “Output 2 lost its connection. Output 1 is unaffected.”
  • “This graphic needs a font it can’t find.”
  • “Deleting drops the graphic that is on air right now.”
  • “6 graphics added. Open Studio to place them.”
  • “Working offline. 3 changes queued.”

Streamatrix never says

  • “Oops! Something went wrong.”
  • “Error: ECONNREFUSED (code 42)”
  • “Are you sure?” with no statement of what happens.
  • “Successfully saved!” — success is silent unless it was in doubt.
  • “Please try again later.”

ClauseAnswersExample

Diagnostics The technical cause is always available and never in the first sentence. “Show log” is a link, not a paragraph.
Marketplace voicecapability, not features

The Marketplace sells what the broadcaster can now do, never a list of files. It uses the language of production, not of software: a package is “a full match-day look for football”, not “6 scenes, 14 layers, 2 fonts”. The inventory belongs on the detail page, under the fold, where a technical director will look for it — and it must be exact when they do.

It never uses urgency, scarcity, countdowns, “only 3 left”, or star ratings out of five. A tool used at the Olympics does not need to manufacture desire, and doing so would tell an operator that the people who made it do not understand their job.

Conformance

the review checklist

A design system that cannot fail a review is decoration. These are the checks any Streamatrix surface must pass before it ships, phrased so that a reviewer who did not design it can apply them.

C1
Every spacing value is one of nine.Inspect the CSS. A number not in the scale is a defect regardless of how it looks.
C2
Every type size is one of six, with its bound tracking.No local letter-spacing overrides.
C3
No semantic hue appears without its meaning.Search the file for the four hex values. Every hit must be on air, attention, cue or health.
C4
Exactly one primary action per surface.If two things compete, one of them is secondary and the screen has not been decided.
C5
Nothing with consequence is delayed by animation.Disable all animation. The product must remain correct and operable.
C6
Every state exists.Loading, empty, error, offline. Force each one; a surface with an undesigned state is unfinished.
C7
Every action is reachable from the keyboard and named in the palette.Unplug the mouse and run the show.
C8
No control exists for a capability the engine lacks.Trace every button to a real engine operation or command.
C9
It survives the gallery test.View at 40 % brightness from two metres. On-air state must be unmistakable, and nothing else should be.
C10
It survives the twelve-hour test.Nothing loops, nothing pulses, nothing moves in peripheral vision unless it needs a person.
14

Known Gaps

stated, not hidden

What Volume One does not yet specify. Written down because an undocumented gap becomes an invented behaviour, and an invented behaviour becomes an inconsistency nobody can trace.

G1
The typeface.Three roles, one system face. The largest single gap; needs commissioning, not designing.
G2
Docking and workspace layout.Drop targets, split ratios, saved workspaces and multi-monitor behaviour are named here but unspecified. Volume Two.
G3
Accessibility.Contrast is checked; a full audit, reduced-transparency handling, and a screen-reader model for the live path are not done.
G4
Density modes.The system is designed at one density. Broadcast desks vary; a compact mode will need its own scale, not a shrink.
G5
Data visualisation.Scopes, meters, waveforms and health graphs have no chart language yet, and they must not inherit the semantic hues.
Volume One ends here Everything specified above is buildable against the engine as it exists today. Nothing in this document requires a capability Streamatrix does not have — that was the constraint, and it is the reason this is a specification rather than a mood board.
