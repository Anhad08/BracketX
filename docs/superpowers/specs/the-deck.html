What changed in this pass

Usage

The graphic is made of parts you can click. Selection is two-way: pick a part on stage and its field lights up; hover a field or a layer and the part outlines. That link is the whole reason to have a stage rather than a form.

Design

Hierarchy instead of uniform grey. The stage is darkest and undecorated with a vignette so it reads as a screen; the deck is a lit surface casting a shadow up over it; chrome recedes. One accent per region.

The tally

Was a 7px dot. Now a housed lamp with a legend that takes the air colour across the whole component — readable at a glance from across a room, which is the only requirement that matters for it.

Keys are visible

Rundown items carry their number as a keycap, and Take shows its chord. An operator learns the keyboard by using the mouse, instead of reading a reference they will never open.

Why the shape is still the point

Press “Show the old shape”. A 16:9 stage in a landscape window is already wide, so a right-hand dock steals the axis the content needs and strands space above and below the frame.

The mode switch replaces section navigation for the core job. Design and Production stop being places you travel between and become two states of one workspace — which is what removes the context loss every time an operator goes to check something.

Known gaps in this pass

Fields are not yet editable in place, there is no drag-to-move on the stage, and the rundown does not reorder. Those are the next things to prototype, and each is a real interaction rather than a picture — worth building only once the shape is agreed.
