Why the shape changed

A 16:9 stage in a landscape window is already wide. A right-hand dock takes width from the axis the content needs most, and leaves dead space above and below the frame — which is exactly what the current build does. Press “Show the old shape” to see the same window with a side dock: the frame gets smaller and the wasted space appears.

Side dock — today

1050 × 590

Stage area 1050 wide, and roughly 200px of vertical nothing above and below a 16:9 frame.

Bottom deck

1364 × 767

The frame grows on both axes, and the space that was dead is now the deck doing real work.

One deck, three instruments

Fill in is a single row: the fields laid out across the width the way a form actually wants to be read, and one primary action. Nothing about 3D, lighting or keyframes is present — not collapsed, absent.

Build spends the width on three panes at once — layers, timing, properties — so a designer stops trading one for another in a 280px column. The timeline is no longer summoned; at this size it fits.

Air turns the same deck into an operator's desk: the rundown on the left, the channel strip on the right, and the stage splits into Preview and Program. No navigation, no scrolling, nothing below the fold.

The mode switch replaces section navigation for the core job. Design and Production stop being different places you travel between and become two states of one workspace — which is what removes the context loss every time an operator has to go and check something.

What this costs

The deck is a real rebuild of the editor shell, not a restyle: panels move from a persistent column into a mode-driven region, and Production's page becomes a deck pane. The theme tokens from the previous prototype drop in unchanged — those two pieces of work do not collide.
